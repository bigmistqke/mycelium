A pane floating over the corner

Nothing selected means no pane at all. It has no resting state to design, because a region explaining that it is empty is a region earning nothing.

Covering something is answerable, taking height is not

An earlier draft ran the pane across the bottom as its own region, on the argument that a floating one covers whatever sits under it. What that costs is permanent: every reading of the graph loses the same band, whether or not anybody wanted the pane.

Floating costs something a reader can undo instead. The graph carries the pane's own height as padding beneath its content, so a box under the pane can always scroll clear, and selecting one that the pane covers scrolls it clear without being asked.

It also keeps the wires still. A pane taking height moves every box when it opens, which makes opening a layout event and every wire a stale measurement until something redraws. A floating one reflows nothing, so loading and resizing stay the only moments that matter.

What moving the scroll costs

Every wire runs between two rectangles the browser measures against the viewport, and the drawing subtracts the grid's own rectangle to turn them into positions on its canvas. That subtraction is right while the grid is not a scroll container, because its rectangle is then the whole of its content.

Once it scrolls, its rectangle is the visible box. Each coordinate needs the scroll offset added back, and the canvas needs the scroll size rather than the client size, or every wire lands where the content used to be.

Looking at the page catches it, which is how every change to this page has arrived: the depth columns, the declaration boxes, the header that survives scrolling. A wire drawn at the wrong offset is the most visible failure this page can have.
