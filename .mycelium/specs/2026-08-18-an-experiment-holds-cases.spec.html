An experiment holds cases, and a case cannot be edited approved 2026-08-18

An experiment holds one script and one reading, so every probe asking more than one thing merges its answers into a sentence. Seven of the nine here join two to four findings behind a semicolon, and the other two use a full stop instead. Not one of them keeps its readings apart.

The corpus already forbids this. A principle it has held since the earlier graph says to read something several times through lenses that differ, and then says plainly not to merge the results, because the disagreement has to survive as disagreement. The single slot makes merging the only option, so the design defeats a rule the project wrote down.

One slot also destroys the comparison the type exists for. Six of the nine have already produced a second, different reading, and each one overwrote the first. A probe earns its keep by disagreeing with what it said last time, and nothing here keeps what it said last time.

A rule could paper over that, and an earlier draft of this design proposed one: replacing a script clears the readings under it. That leaves the correctness of every reading depending on somebody remembering a rule, which is the arrangement this project replaces with structure wherever it can.

An experiment holds cases, the way a specification holds behaviours. The document asks one question and carries the final conclusion. Each case names the lens it looks through, carries the script that looks, and holds the readings that came back.

A case cannot be edited, and that is the whole design rather than a restriction on it. A case is its script, so changing the script makes a different case, and the readings under the old one measured something nobody is asking about any more. Prior art states the same identity for definitions: a thing is its content, so a rename is metadata and two identical things are one thing.

So there is no way to update a case. Adding one appends, deleting one drops it with every reading and conclusion beneath it, and the delete prints what it discards the way deleting an entry already prints the edges it drops. Editing a script means deleting a case and adding another, which costs two deliberate commands and shows the loss in the output of the first.

Readings repeat and carry the day they arrived, newest first. A run appends only when the value moved, so the series records movements rather than runs, and a probe reproducing its last answer leaves nothing behind. The series holds exactly the disagreement this type exists to notice.

Conclusions repeat beside them and carry a day too, so a reader pairs one with the reading it followed. A reading is what the instrument said and a conclusion is what somebody made of it, which is the difference between a measurement and a finding. The document's own conclusion answers the question it asks, and the detail holds everything else: why the probe is shaped this way, what it cannot separate, what it cost.

Creating an experiment with a script still costs one command. Passing a script and a lens together builds the first case, with an id derived from the lens the way an entry's slug already derives from its title. Cases are what somebody reaches for on the second lens, not a toll on the first.

Nothing detects a hand-edited script. A reading could carry a hash of the script that produced it, and an audit could recompute that and report a reading whose instrument has changed underneath it. It is the most faithful reading of identity-is-content and also the most likely to be more machinery than the problem deserves, so it waits for a case of it actually happening.

No cap bounds the series. Nothing records a reading without somebody asking, so growth follows deliberate acts, and a cap invented before a probe has ever grown too long would be guessing at where too long is.
