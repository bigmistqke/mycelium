A claim carries its check into the drawing, because a claim with no proof beside it is an assertion implemented 2026-08-10

The chain page puts real content in its pane for every rank but one. An axiom shows the reasoning under the principle, a specification names the files it answers for, and a declaration shows its doc comment and its whole body. A reader selects a principle and reads down to the code that answers it.

A behaviour breaks the run. readCanon reduces the script inside a canon-behaviour to checks: boolean, and the page spends that one bit twice: the box reads behaviour or behaviour, proved beneath, and the counts line totals how many claims carry a check. The script itself reaches nothing.

So the pane shows a sentence asserting something about the system, and nothing a reader can weigh it against. The rung where a claim stops being an assertion is the one rung the drawing hides.

Twelve of those claims are browser cases in the figure subsystem, each running against a canon-fixture the script never mentions. A reader meeting assert(rowOf(box) === 3) with the markup out of sight gets the assertion and not the case.

The reading widens by one field and loses none. Behaviour carries check, the script's own source, and fixture, the markup a browser case runs against; both are empty when a claim takes its proof from the claims beneath it. Every place that wanted the boolean asks whether check holds anything, so the box label and the counts line keep reading exactly as they do now.

The pane then renders a check the way it already renders a declaration's body: a disclosure, folded, under a summary naming what it holds. The summary says check, which is the word the schema, the validator and the runner all use for the script that falsifies a claim.

A fixture gets its own fold beside it, and only when one exists. The two are different things — the case and what the case runs against — and a reader opening one has no use for the other.

Folded rather than open, for the reason the experiment's method fold already gives: a thousand characters of code between a claim and its neighbours buries everything under it. The claim's own reasoning still reads immediately, since that is what a reader came for.

The disclosure takes its summary word, its language and whether it is a report's section from the caller. Only an experiment's method is a section, so the check keeps the plain summary a declaration's body already has; and the highlighter needs the language because a fixture is markup, which coloured as TypeScript reads as one long string.

The pane also strips the indentation a script inherits from the element holding it. A script sits wherever its element sits, so its lines carry two or six spaces belonging to the page, and trimming the block cuts the first line's indent while leaving every other one. That fix lands in the drawing and not in the corpus, since the markup is right and only the reading of it was wrong.

No verdict on the page. A result belongs to a run, nothing commits one, and every canon-verdict in the corpus reads PENDING by schema. A drawing showing that field would show the same word on every claim.

No running a check from the page. The chain page is a drawing generated from the corpus, and the runner it would need opens Chrome over WebDriver.

Whether a claim proved by the claims beneath it should gather their checks into its own pane. It carries no script of its own, so its pane stays prose, and the reader has to step down to each child to find what actually runs.
