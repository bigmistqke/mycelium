Work interrupts itself. You are inside one thing when you notice another, and starting a parallel session costs more than the notice is worth. The thought needs somewhere to go in one line, and it needs to come back at the moment it matters.

The knowledge graph absorbed this for three weeks and it went badly. Eleven of 578 titles read as a deferral, and two of those have been dead since January: one defers parsing to a v2 that never came, and one defers an implementation this project does not descend from. Nothing said so, because nothing was watching.

Seven of the eleven name no condition at all. Settle the asymmetry in a follow-up is a wish rather than a deferral, and a reader cannot tell whether its moment arrived. The four that do name one point at something checkable: until a second caller wants them, once the count reaches zero, when a check needs it.

The plan family answers a different question. It carries a goal, an architecture, a tech stack and tasks with files and interfaces, which is a design somebody already settled. Twelve documents hold 286 statuses and every one of them says completed, so the family shows what happens when a store meant to drain keeps a declared record of its own progress.

A followup is one intention with somewhere to land. It carries a title, a required link to what it concerns, and an optional condition that would end it. Nothing else.

The name

Backlog was the other candidate and it loses on freight. Grooming, sprints, points and prioritisation ride along with the word, and this design rejects every one of them. Followup is what a person reaching for the concept actually says.

The place is what makes it survive

The link is required because it does two jobs no list can do. It puts the item in front of a reader at the moment they open the thing it is about, so nobody has to read a list to find it. And it dates the item against the repository rather than against a calendar.

every-link-resolves already fails a build on an href pointing at something that no longer exists, across the whole corpus and out to source files. So an item about a deleted file or a retired claim breaks validation. Staleness gets loud instead of accumulating quietly, which is the failure the January deferrals demonstrate.

The link sits inside a followup-at child rather than carrying a data-rel. Containment already says what the link is, so no ninth relation joins the vocabulary, and the audits reading direct-child edges never see it. The canon's own canon-references works the same way for the same reason.

Deletion is the success state

This is the only family here where removing a document means the work went well. A notebook entry is a dated record and an axiom is a claim somebody relied on, so deleting either one throws something away. An item exists in order to stop existing, and the ordinary way it goes is that a person does the thing and drops the note.

Two more forces remove one, and they cover the items nobody acts on. The condition fires, or the place stops existing. Both come from outside the document, which is what keeps an abandoned item from sitting there forever.

Git holds every item this family ever carried, so neither route throws away the history. What the project decided not to do last month stays answerable without a single field recording it.

Doneness is an act, not a state

No status field, and nothing to update. mycelium followup done <file> deletes the file, and the command carries the meaning that a field would try to store.

This is the line against becoming a task tracker. A tracker needs somebody to keep its progress current, and that upkeep is what rots — 286 plan statuses reading completed is what a maintained field becomes once everyone stops. An item that only ever disappears cannot rot in that direction.

A condition may become a script

A condition in prose reads to a person, and that is where this family starts. A condition as a script would answer on request, and mycelium followup due would report which items have come due. That turns remembering into asking, which is the whole point of writing one down mid-work.

The runner belongs to the notebook's experiment type, which does not exist yet, so nothing here ships a script condition. list prints the conditions and a person reads them. The family's own first item records that.

Nothing here will ever run during validate. A condition that fails a build is a gate, and a followup holding a gate would stop work on the strength of something somebody jotted down while thinking about something else.

What an item becomes

A spec, or a plan, or nothing. That is why an item carries no steps of its own: the thing it turns into holds those, and an item that has grown steps stopped being an item some time ago.

Capture has to be one line, without leaving the work. mycelium followup add --at <path> --title "…" [--when "…"] is the whole surface, and --at takes a path from the repository root so a person never has to know where this family stores its files. If capture costs more than that, somebody opens the parallel session instead and the family means nothing.
