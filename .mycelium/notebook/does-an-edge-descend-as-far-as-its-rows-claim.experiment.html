The corpus checks a figure's edges by counting grid rows, because the validator parses with a library computing no layout and every rectangle it measures comes back zero. So the audit can say an edge runs downward and cannot say how far. This is the first probe to ask the second question, and it exists because a probe can now run in a page.

The first reading disagrees with the drawing. Its two nodes sit eighty-two pixels apart from top to top, and the connector's own bounding box reports a drop of eight. A wire joining them has to cross that gap, so one of the two numbers describes something other than the wire a reader sees.

Three readings could explain it and this cannot separate them. The path may carry its own coordinate space, so its box measures units rather than pixels. The selector may have found a marker instead of the connector. Or the drawing may settle after the probe stops waiting, leaving a measurement of a wire that had not finished moving.

Leaving the disagreement recorded is the point rather than a shortcoming. A probe is allowed to be inconclusive, and the next run has something to argue with — which is more than the corpus had about its own drawings an hour ago.
