Headless Chrome measures the figure grid for real, and two engine decisions are already assertable

Headless Chrome 150 measures a figure exactly as a visible one does. The probe loaded docs/reports/2026-08-05-repl.report.html, a figure with thirteen edges, a fork, a merge and a back edge, then read the document back over the devtools protocol.

Every number came out real. The grid resolved to 70.5625px 123.109px 123.109px 123.109px 123.109px: the reserved edge column at auto, sized to the back edge's label, and four node columns sharing the rest. A figure-node measured 123 by 75. All thirteen wires carried coordinates, and none matched the zero shape happy-dom produces.

Two of the engine's own decisions surface in that output, so a test can assert them without reaching inside anything. The two edges out of caller both leave x=320.23 and both turn at y=109.525 — one lane for the fork, which is the whole point of grouping edges into connected components. The back edge runs at x=60.28, inside the 70.56px edge column rather than across the nodes.

requestAnimationFrame fires and ResizeObserver settles. The probe polled until two consecutive reads matched, and they matched on the second. An earlier attempt with --dump-dom --virtual-time-budget never returned, so that hang belongs to virtual time rather than to a redraw loop in the engine. A runner should poll a live page instead of asking Chrome to fast-forward one.

Two flags matter. --no-sandbox is necessary wherever an outer sandbox already exists, which covers both a CI container and the tool that ran this. A --user-data-dir on a scratch profile keeps a run away from the developer's own Chrome.

The probe itself came to about ninety lines with no dependencies: spawn, fetch against /json/list, and the WebSocket global Node ships. Correlating devtools replies to requests by id took fifteen of them.
