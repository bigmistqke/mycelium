The knowledge graph and the canon share no edges: eight prose links and nothing computable

Counted across both: zero edges from a knowledge node into the canon, and zero references from the canon back. Eight links exist, all inside a node's own prose, all written on one day, six of them pointing at the root canon. A reader can follow them and nothing else can.

So the two graphs are disjoint as structures while describing the same work. An axiom settled a design question this afternoon, the decision records that in a sentence, and the axiom has no way of knowing it was used.

One direction is missing and the other is refused, and the difference matters. A decision naming the axiom that settled it is an ordinary edge: the relation vocabulary is shared, and every-link-resolves would check it like any other. The reverse would put history into the canon, which states what holds now and leaves how it came to hold in a dated node.

What the missing direction would buy is a count nobody can take today. How often an axiom decided something, and how often it overturned the move somebody was already making, is the number that separates a lens from a ritual, and answering it means reading prose by hand.
