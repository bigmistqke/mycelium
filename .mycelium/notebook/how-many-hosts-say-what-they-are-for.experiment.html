A proposal to declare a family's purpose in markup met a convention already doing the job badly. Nearly every host opened with a bolded Purpose: label inside a meta block, which somebody wrote by hand and nothing checked. Eleven hosts carried sixteen distinct labels between them, four of which named one concept under four different words.

Two of those purposes sent a reader to something deleted, describing their family as the shape a document takes when written as a v4 node. Version four is the earlier corpus, and nobody can open it. The rule against referring to what a reader cannot reach rides on links, so prose in an unnamed field slipped past every check the corpus had.

The silent one was the canon, the newest family and the one holding what the project claims is true. Whoever arrives last keeps a convention least well, because a newcomer copies a skeleton rather than a habit.

The reading now counts the declaration that replaced all of this, and counts the old label beside it. Both numbers matter while the change settles: the first belongs at every host, and the second belongs at zero. A label reappearing says somebody wrote the description somewhere nothing checks.
