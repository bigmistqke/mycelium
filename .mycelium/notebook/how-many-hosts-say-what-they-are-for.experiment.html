A proposal to declare a family's purpose in markup met a convention already doing it. Nearly every host opens with a bolded Purpose: label inside a meta block, written by hand, checked by nothing. The field exists, and what it lacks is a name and an audit.

Two of the purposes send a reader to something that no longer exists, describing their family as the shape a document takes when written as a v4 node. Version four is the earlier corpus, and nobody can open it. The rule against referring to what a reader cannot reach rides on links, so prose in an unnamed field slips past every check the corpus has.

The silent one is the canon, which is the newest family and the one holding what the project claims to be true. A convention nobody has to keep gets kept least well by whoever arrives last, since the newcomer copies a skeleton rather than a habit.

The population here is a host that declares commands, which leaves out the figure family and the schema vocabulary. Both have types and neither has a command, so a command reading these fields would pass over them. Whether that is right depends on whether the reader wants families or wants verbs.
