Prior art: W3C specification conventions already answer what a behaviour is and how a test cites one

Standards bodies solved most of this chain already, and the parts they solved are the parts that looked hardest here.

  • RFC 2119 keywords. MUST, SHOULD and MAY mark which sentences state a requirement and which give context. That answers what a behaviour is, and it answers it by a word in the prose rather than by anyone's judgement. Exhaustiveness needs a countable list, and this is how the list gets counted.
  • Permanent addresses. Sections and definitions carry ids that outlive edits and never get reused, because the whole point is that other documents cite them. That is the fragment half of an href, treated as load-bearing.
  • Conformance test suites. web-platform-tests links tests back to the sections they cover, across browser engines. The protocol rg built by hand runs in the wild at that scale.
  • Non-normative rationale. Design principles sit inside the specification, marked as not requirements. An axiom may want that place rather than a family of its own.

The project has taken a standard's mechanism wholesale once already: language.template.html models ASD-STE100, down to keeping its rejected words. RFC 2119 is the same kind of object, a handful of words with fixed meanings, and it fits the family that already holds this project's terms.
