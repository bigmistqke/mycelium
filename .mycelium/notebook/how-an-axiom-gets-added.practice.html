Adding an axiom is a commitment to build, because the chain goes red until something reaches it canon harness talk me through the process of creating this new axiom

Somebody asked for an axiom saying structured content beats a convention. Writing it took eight steps, and the corpus forced most of the order. What follows describes the one that landed as root.canon.html#structured-content-over-convention, including three mistakes, since those are where the checks did the teaching.

Check it does not exist

Listing every axiom found the two nearest, and neither was the same claim. One governs a type once somebody declares one, and the other says to derive whatever can be derived. That distinction became the new axiom's third paragraph, so the check paid for itself twice.

Find out what one costs

The grounded audit says every axiom is reached by a behaviour, because an axiom nothing reaches cannot be falsified. So an axiom cannot land alone: the moment it exists the build stays red until something it governs exists too. Adding one is a commitment to build rather than a way of recording a thought, and the tree stayed red on purpose for most of the work.

Measure before claiming

A count came first — sixteen labels across eleven hosts for roughly six concepts, two of them citing a corpus deleted months earlier. That went into an experiment rather than a sentence, so it recomputes. The rule then had something under it instead of merely sounding good.

Write the spec, then the axiom

The design settles into a spec before a source file changes, and the axiom is the claim that spec's design rests on.

Three decisions inside one command

The requester's phrase became the id and not the title, because an id is an address a citation resolves to while a title has to be a claim somebody can falsify. Root's house style is a declarative sentence, so the title mirrors A gate people ignore is not a gate. The required detail took four paragraphs: how a convention fails, how a declaration closes each gap, what the axiom narrows, and what it costs.

Ground it in what already argued for it

One reference went in, then the requester said to search the notebook, and that was the better instinct. Three entries already made this argument from different directions, one of them a case where somebody read a hand-copied list of commands as the complete set. The axiom was not new thinking, and it named four existing entries as one rule.

Then the four labels concatenated into a sixty-three word sentence and failed the sentence-length rule. A standing followup already names that concatenation and defers the real fix until a label limit stops working. It had not, so the labels got shorter and the followup stayed.

Close the chain

Build the thing, add a behaviour carrying the check that falsifies it, link the behaviour to the axiom, and the build goes green.

Three mistakes

The first read a file's reason for needing no conformance declaration as a reason for not checking it, which does not follow, and the requester caught it. The second wrote an audit whose population caught a design document showing a template as an example, when the dictionary already said a host is decided by its filename.

The third is the one worth keeping. The axiom declared its own narrowing as prose inside its reasoning rather than as an edge, and grounded reads direct children only, so the relationship existed for a reader and for nothing that could act on it. The axiom failed its own rule on its first day, and nothing caught it until somebody read the file for another purpose.

One decision still open

The axiom sits in root, and exactly one behaviour cites it, from one subsystem. Where an axiom belongs follows from who cites it, so by the letter this is a subsystem axiom today. Root is a bet that a second subsystem will cite it, and move carries every citation across if the bet does not pay.
