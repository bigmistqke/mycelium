A canon internalised well enough to generate from is a canon nobody can argue with

An axiom shaping which options appear stops being a thing a person weighs. A reasoner generating only what the canon allows never produces the move that would have shown an axiom to be wrong, and the absence looks like agreement.

This session produced the counter-example. An axiom about an edge running through space reserved for it turned out to name a mechanism rather than a criterion, and it moved down a rank. What surfaced it was somebody reading an axiom and asking whether it was really one. A reasoner generating from it would have had no reason to ask.

So the two families answer different halves and the split is worth keeping. The canon stays small enough to reason from, and the graph stays large enough to argue with — a claim, and the record of every time it decided something and what that cost.
