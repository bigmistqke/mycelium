A test sandbox symlinks templates and commands, so writing there edits the real corpus harness canon

A sandbox builds a throwaway tree and links two directories into it rather than copying them. Both links point at the real corpus, so sandbox.write("commands/quiet.command.html", …) writes through one and lands in the project. Two fixture files sat in .mycelium/commands/ afterwards, and the next validate failed on documents no commit had ever seen.

The symlinks earn their place, and the comment beside them says why. A command hosted outside a family lives in that directory, and a tree without it can run an authoring command but not the check saying whether the result validates. Copying instead would leave a test running against a stale copy of the thing it tests.

So the hazard belongs to writing rather than to linking. Every existing case writes under a directory the sandbox makes itself, which is why nothing found this until a case needed a fixture that was a host. Writing anywhere else is safe, and the fix was one path: probe/ instead of commands/.

Nothing catches this. The write succeeds, the case passes or fails on its own terms, and the damage shows up later as a corpus failing on a file nobody wrote.

 a mistake nothing refuses, found only by the damage it left
