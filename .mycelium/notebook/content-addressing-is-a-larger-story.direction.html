An experiment's case arrived at this by one route. A case is its script, so editing the script makes a different case and the readings underneath stop belonging to it. Nothing enforces that, because nothing has to: the identity does the work a rule would otherwise have to remember.

The same move keeps appearing elsewhere in this corpus under other names. A liveness that gets derived rather than declared, a status computed from the steps beneath it, an audit reading the population rather than a list somebody maintains — each one replaces a field that could disagree with the thing it describes. Identity by content is that move applied to the thing itself instead of to a field on it.

Unison takes it furthest of the prior art here, where a definition's identity is the hash of its tree, a rename is metadata, and two identical functions are one function. What that buys is not tidiness. It is that a whole class of questions about staleness stops existing, because nothing can be out of date with respect to itself.

What this corpus would gain is unclear and worth working out rather than assuming. A citation could name what it cited rather than where it sat, so rewording a behaviour would break the citations resting on it instead of leaving them resolving to something the document no longer claims. That is a real limitation the canon currently admits and no check closes.

The cost lands on readers. An address made of content is unreadable, so every place a person types one needs a name beside it, and the corpus is full of places a person types one. Whether that trade pays here is the question, and one followup about a reading naming its instrument is the smallest place to find out.
