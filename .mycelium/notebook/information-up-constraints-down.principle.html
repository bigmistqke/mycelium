Information goes up, constraints go down

The two directions of the chain carry different cargo, and naming that settles which passes are legitimate. Constraints travel down: an axiom settles what a specification may say, and a specification settles what a test checks. Information travels up: an implementation, a test, or a bug reports what is actually there, and that reaches the layer above as evidence.

So deriving axioms from an existing codebase is not the chain run backwards. It is the upward direction doing its job, and it is the only direction available on a project that is already running.

An abstraction cannot cover a complexity it has never met. A specification written from a vacuum states what somebody guessed a subsystem promises, and the guess is not made better by arriving first. Implementation and the complexity it ran into are what inform an axiom worth writing, which is why the practice alternates rather than starting at the top.

The rule this gives for reading a thin spot: an axiom reached by one specification is information, not a construction artifact. It says the corpus exercises little of what that principle governs, and it names where to look next.
