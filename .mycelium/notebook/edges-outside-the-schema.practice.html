The whole knowledge graph sits outside the schema, and two edges use unrecorded relations

The unexpected-child check in the generic validator reads tag.startsWith(familyPrefix) && !allowed.includes(tag). A tag without the family prefix meets nothing. A <div> and a <blink> added as direct children of a knowledge node validate clean.

A census over the 903 instances counts what sits there. 550 <a data-rel> edges and 22 <script> elements, and nothing else at all. So the entire graph structure lives outside the schema: no template says a node may carry edges, how many, or which relations hold.

Two live edges use labels that appear in no vocabulary. 2026-07-23-template-series-built.outcome.html carries data-rel="produced" and 2026-07-25-plan-template-html-complete.outcome.html carries data-rel="resolves". CLAUDE.md names six labels plus specifies and elaborates as deliberate mints, and neither of these appears in either list. Meanwhile blocks is declared and never used once.

That costs more than tidiness, because link upserts on the pair of relation and target. A mistyped relation cannot correct an edge; it adds a second one beside the right one, which CLAUDE.md already warns about and nothing catches.

The scripts are not clutter either. Eleven belong to a test-case and six to a language-rule, where the script is the test or the check itself. The schema cannot see the load-bearing part of either type.

Closing this needs no new vocabulary. Declaring <a repeatable data-rel="^(depends_on|…)$"> in each template uses the attribute-value-as-pattern rule the schema already has, together with the repeatable declaration the cardinality spec adds. The check then drops its prefix guard.
