A thin record has more than one reading, so derivation has no single answer

Loss makes the evidence thin. Thin evidence fits more than one reconstruction. So the same repository yields a different graph on each run.

Non-determinism is the symptom and loss is the cause. The two are one claim rather than two.

A commit that moves three files reads equally well as extracting a module, as fixing a layering violation, or as preparing for a rewrite. Nothing in the artefact settles which. The reader has to pick, and a different reader picks differently.

The consequence worth having: the spread between readings measures the loss. Where independent readings agree, the record sufficed. Where they diverge, the record was thin. So a map of the spread is a map of where a project documented itself badly, which is a claim about the history rather than about the reader.

This predicts something checkable. The loss runs biased toward fast, uncontested work nobody wrote up, so the widest spread should land on exactly that. If it lands instead on the parts a team argued about at length, the reader is careless and the evidence is not thin.

A little spread survives even at zero loss. Turning a complete history into twenty claims is a compression, and what you drop is a choice, so two readers with the same full record still summarise differently. That part is second-order and does not change the mechanism.
