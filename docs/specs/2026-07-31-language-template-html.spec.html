language.template.html: the project's writing rules and terms of art as documents draft 2026-07-31

This project has a voice. Every family has a template that fixes the shape of a document and a validator that checks it, and nothing anywhere fixes the sentences. The rules for those sentences do exist, but they are scattered across places the repository cannot see: a commit-message skill in the user's home directory (plain language, no abbreviations, nothing that needs outside context, present tense, say what and why), a personal memory note (short sentences, no rhetorical framing, no em-dash asides, no bolded mini-headlines, lead with the fact), prose scattered through CLAUDE.md, and the corpus itself by example.

Rules in that state are not followed reliably. The session that commissioned this spec used bolded mini-headlines and em-dash asides throughout, against a note naming both. Earlier, someone wrote eight nodes in one day that averaged 415 characters of title against 117 for the rest of the graph and used the detail field zero times — a shape failure no validator caught, because no validator was looking at prose. The pattern is the one a readers treat a hand-copied command list as the whole tool describes from a different angle: what the harness puts in front of the writer is what gets done, and a rule living somewhere the harness does not reach is a rule that is not in effect.

Terms of art drift with nothing recording it

The project's own vocabulary has no definitions. harness, gate, audit, family, host, drift, instance, conformance all carry specific meanings here, nobody writes down. Nothing stops the next document calling a harness a scaffolding, and nothing would notice.

The one closed vocabulary that does exist — the six data-rel labels, with fixed meanings and a documented procedure for minting new ones — lives as prose inside knowledge.template.html. It works, which is evidence for the approach, but it is not a thing the corpus can see, link to, or check.

Documents link; they do not compose

Every family can hyperlink, and DESIGN.html is explicit that this is where it stops: Not live transclusion. Links point, they don't inline. It lists revisiting that once there's a real corpus to test them against as the last item under What's next. The corpus now exists — 277 knowledge documents, 15 specs, 11 plans — so the condition it was waiting on is already here, without anyone noticing.

A shared vocabulary is a cheaper composable than transclusion, because the unit is the term rather than the passage. A fourth family beside knowledge, spec and plan. docs/templates/language.template.html holds two types, the way knowledge.template.html holds six. Instances live in docs/language/.

The two types

language-rule is a rule about writing. It carries the rule itself, an example that passes, an example that fails, and whether a machine can check it. The passing and failing examples are not decoration: readers read a rule stated abstractly differently, and the pair is what pins it down.

language-term is a word. It carries one meaning, its part of speech, whether the word is approved or rejected here, and — when rejected — an edge to the word to use instead.

The rejected term is the field that does the work. A glossary that only defines approved words documents the vocabulary without preventing anything; what stops drift is recording that scaffolding and tooling both mean harness and are not to be used. This is ASD-STE100's actual mechanism — its dictionary is approved and not-approved pairs rather than a list of entries — and it is the half a glossary usually omits.

Following ASD-STE100's shape, with our dictionaries

The standard is two parts: writing rules, and a dictionary of approved general words with one meaning and one part of speech each. Technical names and technical verbs are explicitly left for each company to supply. Two dictionaries, one general and one your own, is the standard's design rather than a modification of it, and the two types above are that shape.

One deliberate divergence, decided separately in reject closed-world approval: the standard forbids any word not on its approved list, and this does not. Rejected words are listed with their replacements and everything else stays open. Closed-world buys determinism, determinism was for resolving prose to logic, and that ambition is out of scope below.

Enforcement is an audit, not a template

Templates fix the shape of one document. Audits are properties of the corpus and belong to no family — orphans-except-goal and dangling-outcome are already this shape. Voice is a property of every document rather than a kind of document, so it is an audit, and the dictionary documents are the data it reads. No new architecture is required; both halves already exist as patterns.

Term uses are derived, never authored

The audit scans prose and derives the edges from document to term. Hand-annotating each occurrence of validator across 42 documents is exactly the capture cost that killed prior rationale systems, and it would buy nothing that a scan does not.

Deriving also makes the layer un-rottable, which is the argument composition makes decision status derived rather than declared already makes for status: a derived value can be recomputed, so it cannot go stale. A hand-authored use-edge would go stale the first time a sentence was rewritten.

The dictionary cannot bloat, by a rule already in force

A term that derives no uses is an orphan, and orphans are deleted. This is orphans-except-goal applied to a new family, and the same rule link, do not tag set for importing: a node comes in only if it connects to material already here. Usage earns a term its file, so the dictionary self-limits to words the corpus actually uses, and no policy about how many terms are too many is needed.

This is also why a term family is not the tag vocabulary that decision rejected. A tag is asserted about a document from outside it; a term use lives inside the document rather than being declared.

Absorbing the vocabulary that already exists

The six data-rel labels become six language-term documents, and knowledge.template.html links to them instead of describing them in prose. This is consolidation rather than a parallel system: the project's own closed vocabulary, expressed in the project's own primitive, checkable like everything else.

Seeded from rules that already exist

The first entries are a move, not new writing. The commit skill's rules, the plain-language note's rules, and the terms of art measured above are enough for roughly a dozen rules and thirty terms. Writing the template before those entries exist would be guesswork about the field list; writing it after means the fields answer to real material.

The self-hosting test

The dictionary's own documents must pass the dictionary's own audit. If stating the rules in the voice they require is painful, the rules are wrong, and that is worth finding out in the first dozen entries rather than the hundredth.

Resolving prose to logic or to code. The controlled-English survey found that only the logic end of that spectrum resolves, that every working deployment is narrow-domain, and that the dictionary is the whole mechanism. This work keeps the dictionary and abandons the resolver. Nothing here parses a sentence into a meaning.

A closed approved list for general English. Recorded as a rejected option; see the decision cited above.

Transclusion of passages. DESIGN.html holds it as a future direction and it stays there. This composes terms, not text.

Gating on rules a machine cannot check. "One topic per sentence" and "lead with the fact" belong in the template as documentation. They are stated and not enforced, and each rule says which it is.

Question 4 has been sharpened by seeding the first forty-eight entries, which is recorded below rather than as a new question.

  1. Answered. A term's meaning is one sentence, with free-form detail beside it. See the template.
  2. Do the derived use-edges live in a rebuildable index, or should we write them back into the documents? Writing them back makes them visible to every reader and to the existing edge commands, at the cost of touching every file on every scan. An index keeps the documents clean and is invisible until something queries it.
  3. Does the audit gate or report? Gating across 277 existing documents fails on the first run. Gate on node shape, not on logging rate is the nearest precedent and suggests gating only what we write after now, but "newly written" needs a definition that does not depend on git, which a reader of the graph cannot follow.
  4. Which text does the audit read? Seeding produced three concrete cases and they do not resolve the same way, so a single rule will not cover them.
    • language-fail holds an example that must break the rule it illustrates. Every one of them is a violation by construction. Exclude the field.
    • A term may name another term, including a rejected one: make-sure's meaning says it replaces ensure. That is mention rather than use, and a dictionary that could not name a word would be unable to do its job.
    • We wrote the definition of harness using tooling, the word harness exists to replace. A real violation, caught by running the check against the dictionary itself, which is the argument against excluding this family wholesale to make the first two cases go away.
    Code inside <pre> and quoted external material need the same treatment, and quoted material is harder: a quotation legitimately carries someone else's voice.
  5. Do the six data-rel labels migrate in a later pass? Moving them proves the family can hold real vocabulary; deferring them keeps that change separate from knowledge.template.html.
