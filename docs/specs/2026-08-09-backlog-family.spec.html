A backlog that drains: an intention names where it applies, and nothing marks it done draft 2026-08-09

Work interrupts itself. You are inside one thing when you notice another, and starting a parallel session costs more than the notice is worth. The thought needs somewhere to go in one line, and it needs to come back at the moment it matters.

The knowledge graph absorbed this for three weeks and it went badly. Eleven of 578 titles read as a deferral, and two of those have been dead since January: one defers parsing to a v2 that never came, and one defers an implementation this project does not descend from. Nothing said so, because nothing was watching.

Seven of the eleven name no condition at all. Settle the asymmetry in a follow-up is a wish rather than a deferral, and a reader cannot tell whether its moment arrived. The four that do name one point at something checkable: until a second caller wants them, once the count reaches zero, when a check needs it.

The plan family answers a different question. It carries a goal, an architecture, a tech stack and tasks with files and interfaces, which is a design somebody already settled. Twelve documents hold 286 statuses and every one of them says completed, so the family shows what happens when a store meant to drain keeps a declared record of its own progress.

A backlog item is one intention with somewhere to land. It carries a title, a required link to what it concerns, and an optional condition that would end it. Nothing else.

The place is what makes it survive

The link is required because it does two jobs no list can do. It puts the item in front of a reader at the moment they open the thing it is about, so nobody has to read a backlog to find it. And it dates the item against the repository rather than against a calendar.

every-link-resolves already fails a build on an href pointing at something that no longer exists, across the whole corpus and out to source files. So an item about a deleted file or a retired claim breaks validation. Staleness gets loud instead of accumulating quietly, which is the failure the January deferrals demonstrate.

Nothing marks it done

There is no status field, and no command closes an item. Deletion is the only edit, and two forces cause it: the condition fires, or the place stops existing. Both come from outside the document, so nothing here declares progress.

This is the line against becoming a task tracker. A backlog needs somebody to close items, and closing is the act that rots — 286 statuses reading completed is what a store looks like once everyone stopped. An item that only ever disappears cannot go stale in that direction.

A condition may be a script

A condition in prose reads to a person. A condition as a script answers on request, and mycelium backlog due reports which items have come due. That turns remembering into asking, which is the whole point of writing one down mid-work.

The script type is mycelium/experiment, shared with the notebook, because the machinery is identical: a question, code, and a reading. The question differs — a notebook experiment asks what is true and a condition asks whether the moment arrived.

Nothing here runs during validate. A condition that fails a build is a gate, and a backlog holding a gate would stop work on the strength of something somebody jotted down while thinking about something else.

What an item becomes

A spec, or a plan, or nothing. That is why an item carries no steps of its own: the thing it turns into holds those, and an item that has grown steps stopped being an item some time ago.

Capture has to be one line, without leaving the work. mycelium backlog add --at <place> --title "…" [--when "…"] is the whole surface. If it costs more than that, a person opens the parallel session instead and the family means nothing.

Priority, size, assignment, and ordering. Every one of them is a field that somebody maintains, and this project has 286 statuses showing what maintained fields become.
