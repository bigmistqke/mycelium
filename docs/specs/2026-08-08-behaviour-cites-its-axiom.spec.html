A behaviour cites its own axiom, because a subsystem cannot derive from one draft 2026-08-08

The canon hangs its upward edge on the wrong element. A canon-specification carries depends_on to an axiom, and a canon-behaviour carries no edge at all — the schema does not declare one and readCanon hardcodes an empty list for it.

So the corpus says the figure layout engine depends_on documents are the source. A specification names a subsystem, and a subsystem is a grouping of code rather than a claim. Nothing can contradict it, so nothing can derive it from a principle either. Only a claim derives from a principle, and the claim here is the behaviour.

The methodology this family follows puts the citation on each claim-bearing unit, and settles granularity as the unit somebody could later contradict. A spec rule owes at least one axiom, individually. A test owes at least one rule. Nothing owes anything on behalf of a document that happens to contain it.

The damage is visible in what got written. One axiom carries three specifications, which reads as a load-bearing principle and is the opposite: it is general enough to explain anything, so it explained everything and no behaviour ever had to name a principle that fits it. Twelve claims about figure layout hang from a document is the source of truth, which does not predict a single one of them.

The upward edge moves down one level. A canon-behaviour declares <a repeatable data-rel="depends_on"> and names the axiom it refines. A canon-specification keeps specifies, which is a container's honest job — saying which files it covers — and loses depends_on entirely.

grounded then checks two things rather than one, and both are per-claim:

  • A behaviour citing no axiom is freelancing. It asserts something the project never committed to.
  • An axiom no behaviour reaches is dead. Nothing beneath it can push back, so nothing can falsify it.

Reach still runs through narrowing. An axiom a narrower axiom refines counts as reached when a behaviour cites that narrower one, so the walk goes the whole way down rather than one step.

The commands follow. canon link and unlink accept <file>#<behaviour-id> as the source, since a behaviour is now a thing an edge starts from and hand-authoring is what these commands exist to prevent.

The schema also requires a specification to hold at least one behaviour. A container with no claims in it is dead weight the closed-field check cannot otherwise see.

No case layer. The methodology this follows has a rung between a rule and a test, holding derived cases that owe a rule. Nothing here has wanted one, and adding a rung nobody asked for is how a vocabulary grows past what anybody maintains.

No declared scope. That methodology lists in-scope paths in its checker's config, because a citation on every one of several hundred tests would be noise. This corpus has thirty-four cases and total coverage stays affordable, so cited remains total. The warning behind that choice still applies: routine noise from a check means the scope is wrong rather than the suite.

Whether a behaviour may cite more than one axiom. The schema allows it, since a claim can rest on two principles at once, and nothing yet says whether that reads as precision or as hedging.

Whether an axiom should reach a behaviour directly rather than only through narrowing. A general axiom with no narrower axiom under it and no behaviour citing it is dead by this check, which may be right or may just push everyone to write a narrowing axiom to keep the build quiet.
