Reject closed-world approval: list rejected words with replacements, leave the rest open

ASD-STE100 is closed-world. A word not in the approved dictionary may not be used. That is right for its readers: non-native speakers following aircraft maintenance procedures, where a wrong reading is a safety event. It is the wrong trade here.

Closed-world only ever bought one thing, and it was determinism: a fixed lexicon is what lets a parser resolve text to a single reading. That was the resolver ambition, and the controlled-English survey already found it tractable only in narrow domains with a purpose-built dictionary. This work keeps the dictionary and drops the resolver, so the thing closed-world was paying for is no longer wanted.

It also carries the failure that survey names: habitability. A closed lexicon over general English produces text that looks like English and quietly is not, so writers misread sentences they wrote themselves. Open-world with a rejection list is still fully checkable — "does this text contain a banned word" is a lexical test like any other — it just declines to guarantee a single reading, which nothing here needs.
