The value is in the tail, and you cannot tell in advance which thought matters

Imported from the project’s earlier decision log, frozen 2026-07-23.

At the time, the graph held 353 nodes of what somebody judged worth logging — lossy and biased by construction. If capture were automatic, every step would leave a record without anyone exercising discipline: the proposition passes, the probes, the rejections, the failed hypotheses.

Two cases where the value turned out to be in material nobody thought was valuable:

  • The permanently-pending decisions were not logged because anyone thought they mattered. They became the evidence that a retirement concept was missing.
  • Five hand-written tests were never logged at all, and became a measured demonstration that 60% of a test suite was noise.

You cannot tell in advance which intermediate thought will matter. That is the argument for keeping all of them — and the counter-argument, that cheap capture scales whatever quality was already there, is why the graph needs audits rather than just volume.
