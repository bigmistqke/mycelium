Different lenses cover different ground rather than checking each other

Three readings of the same commits produced thirty claims and not one contradiction. That result carries less information than it appears to, since it is equally what truth looks like and what a shared source looks like.

What the readings did instead was divide the ground. The messages lens returned history, meaning what someone tried and dropped. The repair lens returned constraints the code defends. The diffs lens returned structure.

Four claims came from the diffs lens alone, and they are the architectural ones: a hand-built coroutine runner sitting on a synchronous core, resumption split by stage shape, speculative writes running in a separate graph, and atomic commits across sibling bindings. No commit message says any of it.

So dropping a lens costs a category of claim rather than some confidence. The earlier framing treated several readings as redundant looks whose disagreement measures uncertainty. Coverage is the return, and variance is at best a by-product.

One prediction from before the run failed. It said the diffs lens would disagree with the messages lens in at least two places, and the count came to zero. It also predicted claim counts, which could not fail, because the prompt capped every lens at ten.
