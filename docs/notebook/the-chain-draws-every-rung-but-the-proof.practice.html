The chain page opens a pane on whatever a reader selects, and each rank puts real content in it: an axiom shows the reasoning under the principle, a specification names the files it answers for, and a declaration shows its comment and its whole body. A reader can follow a principle down to the code without leaving the page.

One rank breaks that. A behaviour hands the drawing a boolean — checks, true when the claim carries a script — and the script itself never leaves the reader. The pane then shows a sentence claiming something about the system and nothing a reader could weigh it against.

That inverts what the chain is for. Every other rung earns its place by pointing somewhere, and the test is the rung where a claim stops being an assertion. Drawing the assertion and hiding its proof puts the least trustworthy thing on the page and leaves the trustworthy thing in the file.
