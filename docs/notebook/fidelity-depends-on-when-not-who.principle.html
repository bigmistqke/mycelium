A record's fidelity depends on when it appeared, not on who typed it

An agent writing a commit message at the moment of the change has the conversation, the reasoning and the diff in view. That is capture, not forensics. A terse human message someone dashed off in a hurry is the lossy one.

This corpus shows the break plainly. Repositories starting between 2023 and 2025 average 48 to 66 characters a message. Those starting in 2026 average 96 to 242. Authorship changed, and discipline did not.

So a machine-authored history is primary evidence rather than a later summary, and deriving from it is not circular.

One failure mode survives. A contemporaneous message that was confidently wrong misleads every later reader, and they all agree, so the region reads as well documented. Thin evidence announces itself as disagreement. This does not.

That bounds the claim that spread measures loss. Spread measures loss only when the readings look at primary evidence. Several readings of one confident summary agree for the wrong reason.

 the condition spread needs to measure anything: primary evidence, not one shared summary lenses are not independent if they all read the same contemporaneous summary
