A history gives back what got built and removed, not what nobody ever built

Three abandoned designs came back cleanly from pulse's commits: the Awaitable model, the pending brand, and signal write-back. All three shipped and then went away, so each left a trail for a reader to follow.

The design records hold a bigger decision that no reading found. pulse chose not to build a transitions runtime at all, having weighed three ways other libraries do it and turned down each one.

Nobody ever wrote that code, so it left no commits. A reader of the history cannot see a road the project declined to take.

This narrows the claim that a history holds the dead alternatives. It holds the ones somebody built. The ones that died in an argument live only in prose.

 only the built-then-removed alternatives come back; the ones nobody built leave nothing measured against the held-out records
