A parent behaviour takes its proof from the behaviours beneath it and carries no case of its own. A broad claim wanting an end-to-end check gets one as another child, which names what that check asserts instead of leaving it as an extra beside the parts.

The permissive rule looked free and is not. A parent holding both a case and children has two kinds of proof and no rule about which one answers for what, so a reader cannot tell whether the case covers the whole claim or one corner somebody worried about.

The schema states it as a choice: cases, or child behaviours, and never a document holding both. That keeps one sentence describing where proof lives at every depth.
