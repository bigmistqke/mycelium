Read it several times through different lenses, and do not merge the results 64

One reading of a history gives one answer, with no way to tell how much of it the evidence carried. Several independent readings give that, because agreement and disagreement both become visible.

Repeating the same reading is close to useless. It samples the same bias several times and returns the same answer, which looks like agreement and is not. The readings have to differ in what they look at.

Four lenses that genuinely differ: what changed in the code, who changed it and when, what broke afterwards, and what the surrounding issue or pull request said. Each one is blind to what the others see.

Do not merge the results. Union gives noise and intersection gives the obvious. The disagreement has to survive as disagreement, because it carries the information.

Judgement belongs only where the readings diverge. So the primary output is a list of the places the machine could not settle, and the graph is what falls out of settling them.

Not all disagreement means thin evidence. Some of it is one reading being careless. The test is whether it survives more effort, since disagreement that resolves when a reader looks harder was sloppiness, and disagreement that persists is the artefacts staying silent.

This multiplies the expensive step. Reading a diff and judging what it meant is already the part that does not scale over a large repository, and this asks for it several times over. So several readings is something to spend where the answer matters, not a default for every commit.

How many readings before disagreement means anything stays open. Two that disagree tell you almost nothing. How many genuinely different lenses point at the same artefact probably decides the number, rather than picking a count up front.

Agreement across independent readings is a quantity. That is something the confidence field could hold and currently does not, since today it is a number one model picks and nothing reads.
