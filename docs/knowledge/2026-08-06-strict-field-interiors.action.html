Make a field's interior strict, with markup and conforms-to as the two ways out 90 completed

Second and third steps of the strict-content spec, together, because the nested-type case forces it: a <plan-task> placeholder declaring no children would otherwise mean text only and reject every task in the corpus.

A placeholder now says what its interior holds. Nothing declared means text and no elements. markup permits any element, for prose whose shape nobody should fix. Declared children permit those tags with their own cardinality and order. conforms-to="#id" names a type in the same template file, and the parent then carries its children into validation.

The census decided each one. 25 fields hold text and never anything else, so they need no declaration. Seven take markup. Eight declare a short vocabulary, such as language-meaning holding 32 links across 22 instances and nothing besides.

Two things surfaced only by running it. A paragraph declared inside a field is prose, so <p repeatable markup> is where enumeration stops; declaring the paragraph without it rejected every <code> and <q> in the language family. And a test caught the empty-text check returning before the content model was ever read, so an empty element with a required child reported the wrong fault.

Recursion stops at a child carrying its own data-conforms-to, since the crawler finds that child by itself. That is what makes the word worth having: plan-task declares conformance and needs none of this, while a figure's twelve nodes declare nothing.
