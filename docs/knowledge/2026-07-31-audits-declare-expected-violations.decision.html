An audit declares the violations it expects, instead of the engine hiding a directory 90 completed

A template carries worked examples built to fail the audit they illustrate. The engine handed audits a corpus with templates/ filtered out so those examples could not fail the build.

That filter names a location to mean "fixture", and the imprecision costs something concrete: an audit declared in one template file can never see anything declared in another, so a template cannot hold a check about anything but itself. That is the wall the template-audit design hit.

Each audit now names its own fixtures in data-expects, and the engine compares what an audit finds against what it declares. Audits receive every document. Measured before deciding: with templates/ included, exactly three violations appear and they are exactly the three the template's own demo declares. The filter was hiding those and nothing else.

The second half of the comparison is the part that pays. A declared violation that stops appearing fails the run, so the comparison catches an example that stops demonstrating its audit. Nothing checked that before, because nothing looked at fixtures at all.

An audit's own ok is no longer consulted. It reports what it found and the engine decides whether that is acceptable, which is the only way a deliberate violation can be an assertion rather than a failure.
