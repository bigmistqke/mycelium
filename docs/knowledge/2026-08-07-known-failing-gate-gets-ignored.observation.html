The project that ran this methodology before kept its derivation checker out of the blocking gate until every count reached zero. Its reasoning: a gate known to fail teaches people to ignore it, and a check nobody trusts makes its silence ambiguous again, which is the property the whole protocol exists to remove.

That runs against a gap in the chain is an ordinary validation error, which holds that non-zero is communication rather than permission and that a person may ignore it the way a type error does not stop code from running.

One reconciliation is available and worth testing rather than assuming. There the checker reported for a long time before it ever gated, so reporting and gating were two stages. Here validate is one command doing both, which is the arrangement that decision ruled on.

The weight is uneven in a way worth stating. The decision reasons from what this corpus already does, and the counter-evidence comes from people who lived with the arrangement and moved off it.
