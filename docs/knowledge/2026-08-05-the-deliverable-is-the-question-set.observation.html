What derivation optimises for is the smallest set of questions that pins the rest 66

A derived graph will not be right. It does not have to be. It has to be right enough to ask good questions.

So the output that matters may not be the graph. It is the twenty questions only a person who was there can answer, ranked by how much of the graph each one settles. An afternoon spent answering questions rather than writing documents, and the rest follows.

This is the practical form of ratification being the scarce step. If ratification is what costs, then question count is the thing to minimise.
