Naming an axiom for a step already taken is the same failure as checking a design after choosing it, moved inside the reasoning. It reads worse than saying nothing, because the citation makes an unfounded step look derived.

Coverage cannot separate the two. Every step carrying a name is what both look like, and a trace that always agrees with itself is exactly what a post-hoc naming produces.

What separates them is how often the naming changes the move. A trace worth anything contains steps of the form this was going one way, the axiom sends it another. This session had two, and a person applied the axiom both times: the runner belonging to canon rather than beside validate, and a digest of a declaration that git already stores.

So the measure is the rate at which a derivation overturns the step it justifies, not the share of steps carrying one.
