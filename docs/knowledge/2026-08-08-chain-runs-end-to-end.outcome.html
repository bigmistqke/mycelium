Four axioms, three specifications, 28 behaviours and 28 test cases, each citing the behaviour it checks. 1055 documents validate, every case passes, and the canon reaches real code rather than the template's own samples.

A negative test says more than the count does. Retargeting one citation at a behaviour that does not exist drew three separate failures: exhaustive reported the behaviour as untested, cited reported the case as citing nothing, and every-link-resolves reported the href. One mistake, seen from the rung above, the rung below, and the link itself.

The mapping came out one to one because the tests were already written as claims. Every test-name in this repository reads as a falsifiable sentence, so each became a behaviour and each behaviour took the case's own id. Nothing had to be invented to fill the specification, which says the practice was already being followed without a place to record it.

What this does not establish: nothing measures whether the 28 behaviours cover what the three subsystems actually promise. They cover what somebody happened to test. exhaustive asks whether every stated behaviour has a case and cannot ask whether every real behaviour is stated.
