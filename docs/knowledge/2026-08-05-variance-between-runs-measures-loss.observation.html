Variance between derivation runs measures how thin the record is 72

Loss makes the evidence thin. Thin evidence fits more than one reconstruction. So the same repository yields a different graph on each run.

Non-determinism is the symptom and loss is the cause. The two are one claim rather than two.

The consequence worth having: variance measures loss. Where independent readings agree, the record sufficed. Where they diverge, the record was thin. So a variance map is a map of where a project documented itself badly, which is a claim about the history rather than about the tool.

This predicts something checkable. The loss runs biased toward fast, uncontested work nobody wrote up, so high-variance regions should line up with exactly that. If variance instead clusters on the parts a team argued about at length, the reader is careless and the evidence is not thin.

Do not merge the runs. Union gives noise and intersection gives the obvious. The disagreement has to survive as disagreement.

Judgement belongs only where the machine could not resolve. So the real output of a derivation is a map of what it could not decide, and the graph is the byproduct.

This also gives confidence something real to mean. Agreement across independent readings is a quantity. A single number one model picks is not.

A little variance survives even at zero loss. Turning a complete history into twenty claims is a compression, and what you drop is a choice, so two readers with the same full record still summarise differently. That part is second-order and does not change the mechanism.
