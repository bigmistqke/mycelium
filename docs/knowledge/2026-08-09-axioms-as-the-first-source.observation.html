The axioms answer open questions faster than reasoning from analogy does 88

An open question has a cheap first move: ask whether an axiom already settles it. Reasoning from analogy with a nearby subsystem is the expensive second move, and it reaches a different answer often enough to be worth distrusting.

This session ran the experiment by accident. Where the check runner should live came down to an analogy — it exits non-zero and CI runs it, the same as validate, so put it beside validate. The axiom a check lives with its vocabulary answers it directly and the other way: the runner queries canon's own tags, so it belongs to canon. The analogy was to a command that is generic over every template and knows no family's tags at all.

The traffic runs both ways, which is the part worth keeping. An axiom that settles a question cleanly has earned its place, and one that says nothing about a real decision is either too broad or missing. Every step is a reading of the axioms against something that actually happened.
