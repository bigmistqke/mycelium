Architecture is a depth-slice of the intent-to-implementation graph, not a grouping of code 72 active

The question was how to get a picture of a system's architecture out of a derived graph. Two attempts failed before this one.

What works instead: the graph runs from intent at the top to implementation at the bottom, and it passes through every level of abstraction on the way down. A chain might read "users must not lose data", then "writes are durable before we acknowledge", then "fsync before responding", then a function. Four levels, linked.

So altitude stops being a choice you make before drawing and becomes a parameter. The picture at depth two and the picture at depth three are the same object, cut in two places. The question "what level should this diagram be at" does not need an answer.

The edge vocabulary this needs is already named in the graph: implements, achieves and derives_from, relations that connect abstraction levels rather than sitting inside one.

 implements, achieves and derives_from are the edges a depth-slice cuts across the upward walk, from a line of code to the intent above it the downward walk, from an intent to everything that exists because of it
