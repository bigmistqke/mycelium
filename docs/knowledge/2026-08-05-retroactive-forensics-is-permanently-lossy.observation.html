Retroactive forensics is permanently lossy, and lossy in a biased direction 88

An earlier version of this claim said that live logging becomes optional once derivation works. That only holds if derivation can reach live fidelity, and it cannot. You can only recover what somebody wrote down. A decision made correctly in five seconds by one person leaves no trace anywhere, and no amount of compute gets it back.

The loss is not spread evenly. What survives is what someone took the trouble to record, which tracks formality and contention. So a derived graph over-represents contested decisions and under-represents fast confident ones. That is the same bias that sank putting the diagram where the arguments are, arriving from a different direction, which suggests it belongs to the source material rather than to any one method.

This is the standing limit on the claim that the rationale is all already there. Both are true. Where the line between them falls is what caps how good derivation can get.
