Today validate checks everything and prints everything. That suits a corpus where zero is the expected answer and any violation means someone slipped.

The derivation audits change the mix. A half-written chain reports gaps as a matter of course, so the output carries two kinds of line at once: something nobody meant to do, and something nobody has done yet. A reader working on one chain wants that chain, and a reader checking conformance wants the audits where zero is still the answer.

A filter answers this without splitting the command in two, which a gap in the chain is an ordinary validation error ruled out for good reason: one engine, one report, no second instrument with its own ontology. Narrowing what gets printed changes neither the checks nor the verdict.

Open: what the filter selects on. An audit name, a family, a path prefix, or a single document and everything the chain reaches from it are all plausible, and the last one is the most useful and the most work.
