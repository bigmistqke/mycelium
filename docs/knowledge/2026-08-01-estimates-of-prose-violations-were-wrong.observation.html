Every estimate of how much prose broke a rule was wrong until the rule ran 90

Three rules went in today. I measured each one first, to pick a threshold from the corpus rather than from the standard. Two of the three estimates were badly wrong.

Passive voice: I guessed 416 with a pattern. The library found 162. My pattern could not tell is written from is required, so most of its hits were adjectives.

Sentence length: I estimated 211 over forty words. The rule reported 436 on its first run. The real number is 45. Both errors came from how the code split the text, not from the rule itself.

Two separate bugs caused that. The audit joined every block of text with no separator, so two paragraphs read as one sentence. And a list item rarely ends in a full stop, so a run of items read as one very long sentence. That second bug alone accounted for 391 of the 436.

The estimate that held up is the one I did not write. The library measured passive voice within three percent. Every number I produced by hand missed by a factor of two or more.

A pattern over prose looks like a measurement and is a guess. The lesson is narrow and worth keeping: a threshold picked from a hand-rolled count is picked from noise.
