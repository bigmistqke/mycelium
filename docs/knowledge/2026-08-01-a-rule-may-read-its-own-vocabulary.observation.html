A rule that needs the vocabulary is still a rule; the contract was too narrow 88

We wrote the check for replacing words into the audit and into lint rather than as a rule, on the reasoning that a rule is handed the text it checks and this needs the whole family. That is a fact about the contract, not a reason the check is not a rule.

The cost was invisibility. It did not appear in list --type rule, carried no passing and failing example, and was never self-tested. That is the same failure spent all day fixing for audits: a check nobody can see gets duplicated or forgotten.

A rule now receives a third argument, the language it belongs to. That is not arbitrary context and not reaching into the corpus. A rule about words has to know the words, and the words are its own vocabulary.

Nothing is duplicated by making it a rule. The words live in the terms that replace them, so adding one to a term is what turns the check on, and the rule holds no list of its own. Both callers build the vocabulary from the same place and hand it over.

The general shape, and the second time today: a contract that cannot express something is a reason to widen the contract, not to move the thing somewhere it fits worse. The first time was a rule about markup, which could see nothing in a string and nearly became an audit for the same bad reason.
