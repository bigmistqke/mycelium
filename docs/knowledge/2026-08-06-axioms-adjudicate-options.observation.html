An axiom adjudicates options, and the graph has never held that criterion 85

An axiom decides which options are good and which are not. That is its work: a standing criterion someone can appeal to when two designs both function and one of them is right.

The graph has held options and decisions since January, and nothing in it says why an option won. The reason lives in prose inside a decision, or it lives nowhere, and a reader who wants the criterion has to infer it from the verdict. An axiom is the thing an appeal points at, so the edge from a decision up to one turns a rationale into a link.

Where these criteria live today is worth saying plainly: in how a project talks about itself, to its users and to itself. That makes them real but unaddressable, which is the same shape as rationale that already exists and cannot be cited.
