Do not bury a clause between two em dashes; give it its own sentence.

The roster is generated. It cannot fall behind the tool.

The roster is generated — read off the command scripts themselves — so it cannot fall behind.

The paired form is what this forbids: a clause fenced between two dashes, inside a sentence that reads without it. A single dash is a different mark and is not covered. It leads into a consequence or breaks a sentence once, and neither buries anything.

The distinction is worth stating because it was got wrong while measuring. Counting every spaced dash gave 464 across the corpus and made the habit look far more common than it is. Counting the paired form gives 140, plus six dash-separated lists that are not asides at all.

Still a guide. The corpus has 140 sentences someone chose to write that way, and each needs recasting rather than a substitution, which is why the migration is its own pass and not a line in a batch.
