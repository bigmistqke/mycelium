This started as a rejection of tooling and scaffolding in favour of harness, and that was wrong. Both words have a second, legitimate sense here: tooling for software in general, scaffolding for generating a skeleton. Across the corpus, seven of ten uses of tooling and every use of scaffolding were the legitimate sense.

A rejection works on spelling, so it cannot separate two senses of one word. The distinction is real and worth keeping, so it survives here as a rule a reader applies rather than a rejection a machine enforces. That is what machine-checkable: no is for.
