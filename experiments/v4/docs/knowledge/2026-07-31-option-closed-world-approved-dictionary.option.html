Rejected: closed-world approved dictionary, as ASD-STE100 defines it 80

Adopt ASD-STE100's rule directly: a fixed list of approved words, one meaning and one part of speech each, and nothing outside the list may appear. Roughly nine hundred general words in the published standard, plus technical names each project supplies.

Rejected for two reasons. It buys determinism, which is only worth its cost if something is going to parse the result, and nothing here will. And over general English it produces the habitability failure: familiar surface, unfamiliar language underneath, so a writer cannot tell by reading whether a sentence means what it appears to.

Worth keeping on record rather than dropping, because it is the standard's actual design and the obvious thing to copy. The reason not to is specific to having dropped the resolver, and if that ambition ever returns this becomes the right answer again.
