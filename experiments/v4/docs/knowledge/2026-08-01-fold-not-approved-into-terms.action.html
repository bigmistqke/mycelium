Fold not-approved words into the term that replaces them 85 completed

Six words had their own entries saying only Not used here and pointing at a replacement. None was a definition. Each cost a document, an edge, and a required language-meaning field it had nothing to put in.

They are now a comma-separated language-avoid field on the word that replaces them, so use carries utilize, leverage. language-status is deleted with them: a term is approved by existing, which is the third declared field today that turned out to say something the structure already said.

Correction. The commit for this said the merge gives up the reverse lookup that ASD-STE100 is built around, where you look up the word you wrote rather than the word you should have written. That was wrong, and wrong in a way this project keeps arguing against elsewhere: storage form is not queryability. A field is as queryable as a filename, given something to query it with.

So the lookup exists as a query. language list --avoid utilize answers use, and the same data drives a check in both the corpus pass and lint, so writing utilize in a commit message is reported as write use instead. Nothing declares that check and no rule document holds it: adding a word to a term's avoid field is what turns it on, so the list and the check cannot disagree.

It could not have been a rule. A rule is handed the text it checks, and this needs the whole family to know what replaces what. Written as a rule, its own failing example sees no terms and the self-test caught it immediately.
