Spec how a rule carries its own check 85 completed

Two probes ran before the design was written rather than after. A document in dictionary/ carrying a data-audits script was discovered by the existing validator, received all 364 corpus documents, and failed the run with a non-zero exit. A second probe imported acorn by bare specifier from inside that script and it resolved. Both probes were deleted; the corpus is unchanged.

So the design needs no runner, no new field beyond a script, and no project-wide decision about external libraries — a rule that wants one imports it, and a rule that does not, does not.

The part worth keeping is that language-pass and language-fail turn into the check's own tests. They were already required for a human reason. One audit runs every rule's check against its own two examples, so a check that fails to flag its own failing example never reaches the corpus, and no separate fixtures have to be kept in step with the prose.

Five questions left open, of which two are not technical: how much rewriting a sentence cap is worth, and whether advisories are common enough to justify a third outcome in the runner.
