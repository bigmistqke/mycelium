Gate on content sitting outside a declared type, and clear the ten specs that had it 88 completed

Ten early specs carried a closing note in a <footer> beside the <spec-doc> rather than inside it. No field held it, no query reached it, and the schema could say nothing about it, since the schema describes a type's children and this was not one of them. Precisely what overflow prose, outside every field describes.

Eight said Next: and listed the immediate steps, written when the spec was. All are historical now, and one described filtering audit input by the templates directory, which was removed earlier today. Two recorded completion. All ten were snapshots of a moment, which is what work-in-the-now forbids, and the two carrying real evidence are held in the graph already, in the outcome nodes for the crawler and the template series. Deleted rather than moved.

This is an audit rather than a rule in the language family. A rule takes text; this asks about a document's shape, so it needs the DOM. It was on the list of rules to port and did not belong there.

The discriminator turned out to matter more than the check. Template files are mostly prose about a type, with a worked example incidental to it, so almost everything in them sits outside a conforming element quite correctly. Naming the templates directory would have repeated the mistake that declaring expected violations just undid. A document that declares a <template> defines a type; one that does not is trying to be an instance. That separates the two with no directory named, and DESIGN.html falls on the correct side of it without being a special case.
