Forty-eight entries seeded: thirteen rules from the commit-message skill and the plain-language note, and thirty-five terms from the vocabulary measured for the spec. The template commits the family to a self-hosting test, so the banned words were then grepped back over the entries that define them. Three hits, resolving three different ways, which is the finding.

A real violation. The definition of harness was written as "the tooling, instructions and hooks that shape what an agent does" — using tooling, the word harness exists to replace, inside the entry that replaces it. Nothing but running the check against the family itself would have caught that, and it was caught within a minute of the entries existing.

A violation by construction. language-fail holds the example that breaks the rule. Every one of them contains what the rule forbids; that is the point of the field. The audit must not read it.

Mention rather than use. make-sure's meaning says it is the replacement for ensure. A dictionary that could not name a word would not be able to do its job.

The two false hits together are an argument for a narrow exclusion rather than a broad one. Excluding the whole family would silence all three, including the only one worth having, so the audit needs per-field rules. This is spec open question 4 answered by evidence instead of by argument, and it took forty-eight entries to produce, not four hundred — which is the case for building a small family before designing the check that reads it.
