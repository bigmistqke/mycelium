Six hundred links, and until now nothing checked that any of them resolved 90

CLAUDE.md states that a node may only refer to things which exist in this repository. Nothing enforced it. The corpus holds 606 anchors and no check had ever looked at one.

It surfaced by being caught in the act. Folding six words into their replacements deleted the files, and the entry for use named two of them in its own meaning. Two dead links, validate passing, and the only reason anyone noticed was a person reading the file.

The check costs nothing to turn on: all 606 resolve today. That is the awkward part. A gate that passes on the first run has been available the whole time, and the argument for it was already written down as a rule everyone agreed to and nothing tested. A reference into a deleted database sat in one document for two days while the same file claimed there were none left.

It reads anchors from the parsed document rather than matching the source, because prose here describes hrefs. One document explains the same-file convention by writing href="other.html#id" in a sentence, and a source-level match reports it as a broken link to a file that was never meant to exist.
