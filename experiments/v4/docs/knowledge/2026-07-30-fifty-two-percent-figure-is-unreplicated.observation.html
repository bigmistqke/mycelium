The 52% discipline figure was generalised from one night and never re-measured 70

The harness-reframe evidence is this project's founding empirical result: an agent complied 52% of the time with a bolded CLAUDE.md instruction, versus near-total compliance with a hook. Everything v4 enforces rests on it.

The nearest current comparison points the other way. CLAUDE.md carries a comparably bolded instruction to attach the commit hash to the relevant node. In the v4 graph, 53 of 65 action nodes carry one — 82%. Same project, same kind of instruction, four months later.

What this does and does not refute. It does not refute protocols beat discipline — that comparison still holds within the harness-reframe evidence's own session. What it refutes is treating 52% as a stable property of agents. n was 105 commits, one session, one repo, one instruction, and it was promoted to a general law without replication.

Honest caveats, since this is the exact failure the verify gate exists to catch. The instructions are not identical: the harness-reframe evidence's was --commit HEAD at node creation, a single flag; v4's is a follow-up update after the commit exists. The 82% may also be an artifact of the same forcing hook that produced the action nodes in the first place, which would make it a measurement of the harness rather than of discipline. So this is a live tension, not a clean replication — which is the point: nothing in the graph currently records that a founding number is contested.
