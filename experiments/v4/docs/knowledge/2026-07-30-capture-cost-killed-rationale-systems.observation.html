Design rationale is the direct ancestor of this project, and its documented failure mode is the one constraint that matters here.

Design rationale methods can be intrusive in the design process or can be not cost-effective when the designers bearing the costs are not the same as the benefiting persons.
the documented knowledge can be of low quality, e.g., outdated, which impedes its use.

Thirty years of research — IBIS, gIBIS, Compendium, QOC — and what sank it was the asymmetry between who pays to write and who gains by reading. ADRs are deliberately lightweight because full rationale capture failed.

What changed here is that the capturer is a machine. That is the specific reason deciduous #350 and #364 (derive the graph, don't remember it) are not an optimisation but the thing that makes any of this viable.

But it moves the problem rather than solving it. If capture is cheap, volume explodes, and the literature's second finding bites harder: outdated documented knowledge impedes use. Cheap capture does not produce quality, it scales whatever quality was already there. That is the argument for aiming audits at unchecked and superseded claims rather than at volume.
