Rename the language family to language draft 2026-08-01

The family called dictionary holds 35 terms, 1 rule and 13 guides. Two thirds of it is not a dictionary.

The name shapes how the thing is read. A dictionary is a word list, so the rules and guides read as an appendix bolted to one. They are the opposite: the rules are the only part that enforces anything, and the terms are the part a machine has never checked. Calling the whole family after its least active third puts the emphasis in the wrong place.

ASD-STE100, the standard this borrows its shape from, is a language specification. Its dictionary is Part 2. The writing rules are Part 1, and the two together are what makes it a language rather than a glossary. That is the same split this family already has, under a name that only covers half of it.

The project has been calling it the language spec in conversation for a day, while the file is named for the dictionary. That gap is the thing being closed.

One family, renamed. Three types, unchanged in content: language-term, language-rule, language-guide.

Instance filenames do not change. They carry the type, not the family: harness.term.html stays harness.term.html. That is what already lets a command derive an entry's directory from its own name, and the rename does not touch it.

One family, not two

The alternative was to split: keep dictionary for terms and give rules and guides a family of their own. They share only title and detail, and they already sit in separate directories, so the seam is real.

Rejected. The two halves are one thing in use. A rule that says to write harness rather than tooling is meaningless without the terms it names, and the terms exist to be enforced by the rules. Splitting them would put a family boundary through the middle of a single argument, and this project has one cross-family import in it, deliberately, because a boundary is expensive to cross.

The references are the work

The retagging is mechanical. The references are not.

  • 19 hrefs point into the family. These must be repointed. A node may only refer to things that exist in this repository, so a link to a file that no longer exists is not a stale mention, it is a broken one.
  • 23 knowledge nodes mention the dictionary in prose. This is the judgment.
  • 2 specs name the family, including one whose own title is about it.

For the prose, the rule is the use and mention distinction that has come up twice already today. A node saying the language family names a thing that still exists under a new name, so it is renamed. A node saying a dictionary that only defines approved words prevents nothing uses the ordinary word and is left alone.

A node's own title is renamed on the same rule. Renaming does not falsify the record: the work happened to a family that still exists, and a record pointing at a name nothing answers to is worth less than one that resolves.

Order

  1. Move and retag the 49 instances. Nothing outside points at them yet in the new location, so this fails loudly and in one place.
  2. Rename the template and its stylesheet, and retag every type and field inside.
  3. Repoint the 19 hrefs.
  4. Rename the audit.
  5. Prose, in the two specs and the 23 nodes, by the use and mention rule above.

Each step leaves validate failing until the next, which is the point: the corpus is the test, and a rename that passes at every intermediate step is one where something is not being checked.

Any change to the types. Same three, same fields, same rules about which carries a check. This renames and moves; it decides nothing about content.

An alias for the old command name. There is one caller, and it is a person typing it. A second name for one thing is the drift this project keeps finding.

Splitting into two families. Rejected in the design section.

Whether dictionary earns a term entry of its own. It is a word this project uses with a specific meaning, so it probably does, but that is an entry rather than part of a rename.

  1. Does the family prefix on fields carry its weight? Every field becomes language-title, language-meaning and so on, which is long and repeats a word the directory already says. The convention exists so a field belongs to a family rather than to one type, and it has never been questioned. This rename touches every field in the family and is the cheapest moment to ask.
  2. What happens to the specification named for the dictionary? 2026-07-31-language-template-html.spec.html names the family in its filename, and a spec filename carries its date and topic. Renaming the file changes an address; leaving it means a spec named for something that no longer exists.
  3. Is a term still a term? Under a language family the word is fine. But language-term and language-rule read as though a rule is not made of terms, when a rule about a word is exactly that.
